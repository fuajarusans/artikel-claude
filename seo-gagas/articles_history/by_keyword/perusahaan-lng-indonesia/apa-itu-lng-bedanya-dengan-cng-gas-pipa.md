# Article notes

## Angle summary
This is the LNG-primary definitional piece that establishes LNG as the subject, framing it alongside CNG and gas pipa as three modalities for delivering the same gas bumi (natural gas). Unlike the CNG cluster's comparison of three gas types (CNG/LNG/LPG) by origin and composition, this article focuses on three *distribution modes* of the same gas, differentiated by how it is densified (cooling to −162°C for LNG, compression ~200–250 bar for CNG, no densification for pipa) and the economic logic of each mode. The angle is *definitional + comparative*, teaching decision-makers at the awareness stage that LNG, CNG, and gas pipa are variants of one resource, not three separate fuels.

## Subtopics covered
- Definition of LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas): gas bumi /metana (CH4) cooled to ~−162°C, volume shrinks ~1/600
- Why gas must be liquefied: densifying for transport and storage over distance/without pipeline
- Critical temperature physics: metana's critical temperature ~−82.6°C explains why cooling (not just compression) is necessary
- CNG: compressed ~200–250 bar, stays gas, volume ~1/250
- Gas pipa: no densification, continuous flow through fixed network
- Three modalities of the same gas source: liquefaction, compression, pipeline
- Regasification: LNG must be reheated to gas before use (CNG only needs pressure reduction)
- Table: side-by-side comparison of wujud, mode of densification, volume ratio, transport infrastructure, best use case
