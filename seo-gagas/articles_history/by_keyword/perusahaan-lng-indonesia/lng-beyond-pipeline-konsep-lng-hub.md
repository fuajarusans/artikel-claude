# Article notes

## Angle summary
This article introduces the LNG Hub as the infrastructure node for distributing LNG to customers beyond the pipeline network in Indonesia. The focus is *operational/conceptual*: what is an LNG Hub, how does break-bulking work, what is the virtual pipeline (ISO tank + LNG truck modality), and when does this approach make sense for HOREKA and mid-scale industries. Unlike article 01 (which is purely definitional across three modalities), this piece explains the *logistics chain* that makes LNG reach end-users in unpipelined regions. It teaches break-bulking, boil-off gas, vaporization, and LNG Hub as a simpul distribusi—positioning Gagas not as a producer but as the operator of such a hub in the downstream distribution layer.

## Subtopics covered
- Definition of LNG Hub: simpul distribusi (distribution node) receiving large LNG volumes and breaking them into smaller units (ISO tank, LNG truck) for retail delivery
- Geography rationale: Indonesia's archipelago makes full pipeline build-out uneconomical; policy recognizes LNG and CNG as official distribution modes (RIJTDGBN 2022–2031)
- Seven-step virtual pipeline process: liquefaction → storage/loading → transportation (LNG carrier/ISO tank) → terminal regasification/FSRU → break-bulking at LNG Hub → transport to customer → vaporization at site
- Break-bulking concept: splitting bulk LNG cargo into retail units
- LNG ISO tank: 20,000–40,000 liter criogenic containers, double-wall vacuum insulation, holds −162°C
- Boil-off gas (BOG): slow evaporation during transport/storage, requires management
- Virtual pipeline: LNG truck distribution as substitute for fixed pipeline
- HOREKA and mid-scale industry as end-users: hotels, hospitals, restaurants, manufacturing outside pipa
- When LNG Hub is NOT the best answer: location already on pipa, volume too small (CNG better), rantai dingin not available
