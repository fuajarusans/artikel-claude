# Article notes

## Angle summary
This is a *decision-making framework* article that directly compares LNG vs CNG for industrial users beyond the pipeline network. Unlike article 01 (three modalities education) or article 02 (LNG Hub logistics), this piece is the *buying guide*: given that you need gas without pipa, should you choose LNG or CNG? The framework rests on four axes: volume/baseline consumption (LNG for high & continuous; CNG for small–medium), distance from source (~300 km as rule-of-thumb threshold where LNG becomes economical), available space for criogenic infrastructure, and desired delivery frequency. The decision tree is vendor-neutral and practical; Gagas is credible because it distributes *both* modalities and can compare without bias. The honest H2 ("When LNG is NOT yet suitable") builds trust.

## Subtopics covered
- LNG and CNG as two ways to densify the same gas bumi without pipa
- Density difference: LNG ~1/600 shrinkage; CNG ~1/250 shrinkage → ~2–3× energy density per volume for LNG
- Four axes of decision:
  1. Volume/baseline consumption: high & continuous favors LNG; small–medium favors CNG
  2. Distance from source: ~300 km as rough industry global threshold (NOT Indonesian regulation); >300 km LNG more economical
  3. Available space for criogenic tank at customer site
  4. Delivery frequency: LNG denser → fewer deliveries needed
- No single volume threshold; decision depends on combination of factors
- Boil-off gas (BOG) as operational complexity of LNG
- Infrastructure at customer: LNG requires vaporizer/regasification unit; CNG needs only PRS (pressure reduction)
- Honest section: when LNG is NOT suitable (volume too small/intermittent, no space, pipa already close or coming soon, team unready for cold-chain discipline)
