# Article notes

## Angle summary
The tender is won, basic permits are in hand, then one question emerges that no procurement document addresses: is this financed from the sponsor's balance sheet or through a separate entity that stands on project cash alone? That choice determines who bears the risk if the project defaults, how long financing takes to negotiate, how expensive the capital is, and whether there's room left on the balance sheet for the next project. References in Indonesian circulation mention the three routes—pemerintah financing, corporate financing, project financing—but stop at the name. No page converts that into a decision framework with usable thresholds. The differentiator is a side-by-side comparison: three paths with pros/cons, the metrics lenders actually evaluate (DSCR, tenor, debt-to-equity), and the honest answer to "when this structure isn't worth the cost." PT PII's credibility is specific: government guarantees are designed to work on project finance structures—structures most dependent on contractual certainty from the government's side.
