# Article notes

## Angle summary
The tender is won, the cooperation agreement is signed, and the project team thinks the heavy lifting is done. Nine months later, funds still haven't landed. This pattern repeats because credit committees evaluate projects with questions different from those on procurement documents. Lenders don't ask "how much is the asset worth?"; they ask "how reliable is the cash flow for twenty years?" This article reverses the vantage point: instead of explaining how to apply for financing, it unpacks what's actually tested from across the desk, which signals move the cost of capital up or down, and why a government guarantee doesn't close every reason a lender might walk away. The competitive gap is clear: almost no content addresses this—most material is either "how to apply" (framing the borrower's problem) or "risk management" (general). The specificity of the lender's chair is almost never captured. PT PII's credibility is structural: government guarantees are precisely designed to move government-side risk out of the lender's calculation, which is the correct mechanism and the mechanism tested here.
