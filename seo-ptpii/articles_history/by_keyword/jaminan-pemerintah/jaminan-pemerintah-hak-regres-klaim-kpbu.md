# Article notes

## Angle summary
One question is rarely asked in project prep meetings despite determining a region's budget five years out: if a claim is actually paid, where does the money stop? Answer that shocks many PJPK: it doesn't stop. The guarantor pays the Badan Usaha Pelaksana first, then recoups from the PJPK via hak regres. A government guarantee moves the timing and certainty of payment, not the ultimate responsibility. The competitive gap is acute: hak regres is a live related search (genuine demand), yet no popular page answers it for practitioners. The one serious competitor (Kemenkeu article on ring-fencing APBN) is image-format, truncated, without dated figures, and does not touch regres or claim flow at all. The differentiator is the operational claim flow (reconstruction from practice, explicitly not pasal quotes) and the ex-post vs ex-ante distinction (ex-ante = risk allocation; ex-post = regres and repayment). PT PII's credibility is structural: regres is part of the guarantee architecture it issues as BUPI, and APBN ring-fencing is one reason it was created.
