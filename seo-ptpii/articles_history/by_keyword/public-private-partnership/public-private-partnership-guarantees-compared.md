# Article notes

## Angle summary
International sponsors arrive in Indonesia with a risk-mitigation vocabulary already built (MIGA political risk insurance, World Bank partial risk guarantees, ADB credit guarantees, export credit agency cover). They need to know where Indonesia's own government guarantee sits in that matrix: does it substitute, stack alongside, or underlie those instruments? The competitive gap is stark: World Bank and MIGA pages describe their own instruments; Indonesian sources describe theirs; no page puts them in one table. The differentiator is a genuine structural insight from the World Bank's own material that surfaces rarely in Indonesian coverage: insurance-type political risk cover (MIGA) typically requires a waiting period and arbitration before paying, while bank-type partial risk guarantees from MDBs do not—a difference that changes liquidity risk pricing on a debt service payment. PT PII can speak without overreach because it is itself externally rated, works alongside multilateral institutions on large projects, and is the issuer being benchmarked.
