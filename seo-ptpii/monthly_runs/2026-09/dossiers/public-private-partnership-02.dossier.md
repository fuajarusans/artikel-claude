# Research Dossier: Public Private Partnership Step-In Rights: Do They Work?

*Language note: this dossier and its FAQ answers are in English per the brief. Indonesian regulation and entity names appear in Indonesian with an English gloss on first use.*

## TL;DR for the writer

- **Keep two "step-ins" apart.** (1) The **lender's** step-in right — a private-side, contractual remedy built through a **direct agreement**, designed to prevent termination, not transfer ownership. (2) The **contracting agency's** (PJPK's) own right, under the KPBU agreement itself, to terminate and resume control on an uncured default by the Badan Usaha Pelaksana (BUP) — a government-side remedy that exists independently of any lender arrangement. A government guarantee is a *third*, separate mechanism again (see below). The brief is right that these get conflated — don't let the draft do it.
- **The honest finding survives verification, with a caveat on its year.** Global Infrastructure Hub (GI Hub), reviewing a 250-project global sample, found "no example of substitution" — a direct, quotable line. State it as "a Global Infrastructure Hub review of 250 PPP projects worldwide," not "step-in never works" and not "in [a specific year]" unless the editor independently confirms the study's publication year (I could not pull it from the page — see Flags).
- **Indonesia's legal texture is the sharpest, most defensible claim in this article.** Step-in is built through **conditional assignment (*cessie*)** — a contractual arrangement, not a recognised, registered security interest under Indonesian law. Multiple independent Indonesian legal-practice sources describe this approach as commonplace but **untested before Indonesian courts**. Any substitute BUP must clear the contracting agency's approval (eligibility, nationality, track record, technical and financial capacity).
- **Perpres 38/2015's own text does not spell out termination-compensation or substitution mechanics** — I confirmed this against the primary text itself. Those matters live in the individual KPBU agreement. Do not pin a pasal number to step-in or substitution; describe it as agreement-level, not statute-level.
- **Fitch requires careful handling — resolved, but with a caveat you must carry into the draft.** PT PII's own 'BBB'/'F2'/'AAA(idn)' affirmation, Outlook **Stable**, is dated **12 February 2026**. Separately, Indonesia's **sovereign** outlook was revised from Stable to **Negative on 4 March 2026** — this is now **confirmed via Bank Indonesia's own official site** (not just media reports, which is what the strategist's cache flagged as unresolved). Because PT PII's rating is explicitly credit-linked to the sovereign, **do not write "PT PII's outlook is Stable" as an unqualified current fact.** Date the 12 February affirmation explicitly; if the sovereign move is mentioned at all, date it separately; safest default is to cite only the letter grades with their 12 February 2026 date and skip the word "outlook."
- **No documented Indonesian instance of step-in or substitution being exercised was found.** Treat this as absence of evidence, not proof of permanent non-occurrence — Indonesia's KPBU deal count is a fraction of GI Hub's 250-project sample, and outcomes on individual projects are not routinely disclosed.

## FAQ Q&As (7 drafted, English)

### What are lender step-in rights in a public private partnership?
Lender step-in rights are a contractual right, set out in a direct agreement between lenders, the contracting authority and the project company, allowing lenders or their nominee to intervene in or substitute the project company before a default leads to termination. A Global Infrastructure Hub study of 250 PPP projects worldwide found the mechanism is designed to prevent termination, not to transfer ownership.

### What is a direct agreement, and why does it matter for step-in rights?
A direct agreement is the tripartite contract linking lenders, the contracting agency (PJPK, Penanggung Jawab Proyek Kerja Sama) and the project company. It commits the agency not to terminate the underlying cooperation agreement until lenders have had a chance to cure a default. Without it, a lender's step-in right has little practical force, since the authority owes the lender no direct obligation to pause termination.

### What actually happens when a PPP project company defaults?
In standard international practice, a defined default event triggers notice to the contracting agency, followed by a standstill or cure period during which lenders may intervene directly or nominate a substitute operator. Any substitute typically needs the agency's approval. If the default is cured, the project continues; if not, the agreement proceeds to termination and compensation under the cooperation agreement's own terms.

### Can a foreign lender legally take over an Indonesian project company?
Not straightforwardly as an ownership transfer. Indonesian legal commentary characterises step-in as a conditional assignment (*cessie*) — a contractual arrangement rather than a registered security interest — and any substitute entity must clear the contracting agency's approval on eligibility, nationality, track record, technical and financial capacity. Sector foreign-ownership limits can further narrow who qualifies.

### Are step-in rights enforceable under Indonesian law?
They operate as contractual rights rather than a recognised form of statutory security, so enforceability rests on the direct agreement's drafting and the contracting agency's cooperation rather than on registering a lien. Indonesian legal commentary notes that using an assignment for security purposes is, in practice, common but has not been tested before Indonesian courts, which creates residual uncertainty offshore lenders should price into diligence rather than assume away.

### Are lender step-in rights ever actually exercised?
Rarely, on the evidence available. A Global Infrastructure Hub study covering 250 PPP projects worldwide found no example of a lender actually substituting the project company; step-in functions mainly as a negotiating lever that keeps the authority from terminating early. This research found no publicly documented instance of step-in being exercised on an Indonesian KPBU (Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha) project either, though that reflects limited public disclosure as much as it reflects rarity.

### Does a government guarantee substitute for a lender's step-in rights?
No. A government guarantee, such as the guarantees PT Penjaminan Infrastruktur Indonesia (PT PII) issues as Indonesia's Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur, responds to the contracting agency's own default or payment obligations under the cooperation contract. Lender step-in responds to the project company's performance failure. They are separate mechanisms for separate failure modes, and neither cures the other's gap.

## Data tables for the writer

### The default-to-outcome sequence (H2 #3 spine — numbered list, not a table)
1. **Default event** — the project company breaches a defined trigger under the KPBU cooperation agreement (e.g., missed construction milestones, payment default, an insolvency-related event).
2. **Notice** — formal notice of the default is given to the contracting agency, as required under the direct agreement.
3. **Standstill / cure period** — the contracting agency holds off terminating the cooperation agreement while lenders exercise their cure rights; duration is negotiated per project — **no sourced general standard duration found this session; do not invent a day-count** (see Flags).
4. **Lender intervention or nomination of a substitute** — lenders step in directly (via a nominee) or propose a substitute entity to take over performance.
5. **Contracting-agency approval of the substitute** — the nominated entity must clear eligibility criteria (nationality, track record, technical and financial capacity, and any sector ownership limits).
6. **Outcome** — either (a) the default is cured and the project continues under the original or substitute operator, or (b) cure fails and the agreement proceeds to termination and compensation under the cooperation agreement's own terms.

### What offshore lenders often assume vs. what Indonesian practice delivers (H2 #4 mandatory table)
| What lenders often assume | What Indonesian practice actually delivers |
|---|---|
| Step-in is a perfected security interest over the project company | Step-in is built through conditional assignment (*cessie*) — a contractual right, not a registered security interest, and its validity as "security" is untested before Indonesian courts |
| A lender or its nominee can step in unilaterally once default occurs | The direct agreement typically requires notice to the contracting agency and the agency's forbearance from terminating while lenders act |
| Any qualified financial institution can nominate a substitute | A substitute must clear contracting-agency approval — eligibility, nationality, track record, technical and financial capacity — a live constraint where sector foreign-ownership limits apply |
| Step-in is a frequently used remedy, so the legal spend is "insurance" that gets used | A 250-project global review found no substitution instances at all — step-in functions mainly as a preventative negotiating lever, rarely as an executed transfer |
| The clause travels the same way across jurisdictions | Indonesian civil-law rules on security (fiducia, hak tanggungan, etc.) have no direct statutory equivalent for assigning a concession contract as security, so the mechanism rests on contract law and negotiated forbearance |

### Three mechanisms that get conflated — keep them separate
| Mechanism | Who holds/exercises it | Triggered by | What it does | What it does NOT do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lender step-in / substitution | Lenders, via the direct agreement | Project company default under the KPBU agreement | Lets lenders intervene or nominate a substitute to cure the default and avoid termination | Transfer ownership/equity to the lender; guarantee repayment of the debt |
| Contracting agency's own termination-and-takeover right | Contracting agency (PJPK) | Uncured default by the Badan Usaha Pelaksana (BUP) | Lets the agency terminate the agreement and resume control of the asset, subject to the agreement's compensation terms | Protect the lenders' position directly — this is a separate, government-side remedy, not a lender tool |
| Government guarantee (e.g., PT PII's Penjaminan Pemerintah) | PT PII, as counterparty to the PJPK | The PJPK's own default or specified government-side risks under the cooperation contract | Backs the government's payment/compensation obligations, supporting bankability | Cure a project company's operational or commercial failure; act as a party to the lenders' direct agreement |

## Edge cases & nuance

- **Step-in is not acquisition.** It does not transfer equity or ownership of the project company; it is an operational/contractual intervention aimed at curing a breach, then typically handing control back.
- **Three mechanisms, three failure modes** — see the table above. A government guarantee does not cure a sponsor's commercial or performance failure, and lender step-in does not touch the government's own payment obligations. Writers should resist the urge to blend these into one "protection" narrative.
- **Contractor-level insolvency is far more common than project-company-level insolvency** and is normally absorbed by the contractor's own security package (performance bonds, parent-company guarantees), not by invoking lender step-in.
- **The legal-fragility point cuts one way, not the other.** It is a genuine, sourceable reason lenders negotiate step-in hard — but the honest framing is "it works as a negotiating and forbearance tool, and its status as a perfected security interest is untested," not "step-in doesn't work in Indonesia."
- **Absence of a documented Indonesian substitution case is not proof it has never happened.** Indonesia's KPBU deal count is a fraction of GI Hub's 250-project global sample, and commercial/legal outcomes on individual projects are not routinely disclosed publicly.
- **Cure-period and standstill-period durations are project-specific and negotiated.** No sourced general Indonesian (or global) standard duration was found this session — do not invent a number of days or months.
- **PMK 68/2024 governs the government-support architecture (guarantee, VGF, Availability Payment, project preparation), not step-in mechanics** as far as this research could confirm. Keep the two separate in the article.

> Step-in rights are a lender's contractual right, created through a direct agreement with the contracting authority, to intervene in or nominate a substitute for a defaulting project company before the underlying cooperation agreement is terminated.
