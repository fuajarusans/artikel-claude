# Public Private Partnership Co-Financing: ECAs, DFIs, Banks

*(58 characters. Primary keyword occupies the first 26 characters. Entity-rich for AI Overview extraction — the title names three lender classes explicitly.)*

## Hook hypothesis

A foreign sponsor wins preferred-bidder status on an Indonesian PPP and then discovers the harder problem: the project needs around a billion dollars of debt, and no single institution will write that ticket. The debt has to be assembled — an export credit agency tranche tied to where the turbines were built, a development finance institution taking construction risk that commercial banks will not, and a local bank supplying the rupiah that matches the project's actual revenue currency. Each of those lenders arrives with its own eligibility rules, its own conditions precedent, and its own view of who gets paid first.

Almost nothing written in English about Indonesian PPP explains this assembly. The August cluster covered what lenders check and how instruments compare; the SERP's strongest cross-border page (A&O Shearman) covers the framework and government support but, verified this run, does not address co-financing lenders at all. That is the gap: not *whether* a project is bankable, but *who funds which slice and on what condition*.

PT PII can speak to this credibly because the government guarantee sits underneath every one of those tranches without belonging to any of them. It is the institution that has to make one guarantee instrument work for a lender group with mismatched requirements — which gives it a vantage point on where cross-border stacks actually break.

## PPP / guarantee topic to teach (vendor-neutral)

Teach the composition of an infrastructure capital stack and the distinct roles of export credit agencies, development finance institutions and commercial banks as *funders*. Teach that the Indonesian government guarantee is issued in respect of PJPK obligations under the KPBU contract and is not a repayment guarantee to lenders. Teach the currency-matching problem plainly.
