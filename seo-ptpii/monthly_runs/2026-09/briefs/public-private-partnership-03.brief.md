# Public Private Partnership: Indonesia vs Regional Frameworks

*(60 characters. Primary keyword occupies the first 26 characters. **The keyword must be spelled out, never abbreviated to "PPP vs" — see the intent hazard below.**)*

## Hook hypothesis

A regional infrastructure allocator with a mandate across Southeast Asia has to answer a question no single-country guide addresses: the same project, sponsored the same way, sits inside three quite different state architectures depending on where it lands. Indonesia answered the problem by creating a separate, capitalised guarantee company with a ring-fenced balance sheet. The Philippines wrote government undertakings into statute and backed them with a national risk-management fund under its 2023 PPP Code. Vietnam codified revenue sharing directly in its PPP Law, with defined upside and downside thresholds. Three institutional answers to one question — who stands behind the state's promises, and from what balance sheet.

Comparative material either sits in a 2015-vintage ASEAN survey or is split across single-country law-firm guides. Nothing current puts the three architectures side by side for someone deciding where to deploy. That is the gap.

PT PII can speak to this without overreach precisely because Indonesia's answer *is* the institution — it is the ring-fenced balance sheet in question. The obligation that comes with that is scrupulous fairness: the other frameworks are described accurately and neutrally, with no suggestion that Indonesia's model is superior. The article's honest conclusion is that the architecture matters less than the delivery.

## Article skeleton — H2s the writer should expand

Each H2 opens with a 40–60-word direct answer before elaboration (GEO §2a).

1. **H2 (definitional)**: "What is a national public private partnership framework?" — *covers*: the statutory basis, the responsible institution, the forms of government support available, and the mechanism for managing the resulting contingent liabilities; why frameworks diverge even where the projects look identical.
2. **H2**: "Indonesia: a separate guarantee company with a ring-fenced balance sheet" — *covers*: Perpres 38/2015 as the KPBU basis; Perpres 78/2010 establishing guarantee through a Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur; PMK 68/2024 consolidating infrastructure guarantee, VGF, availability payment and project-preparation facilities; the single-window principle; that ring-fencing is the defining design choice, insulating the state budget from claim shocks.
3. **H2**: "The Philippines: statutory undertakings and a risk management fund" — *covers*: Republic Act No. 11966, the PPP Code, signed 5 December 2023 and effective 23 December 2023; government undertakings including guarantees on demand, on private-sector return and on loan repayment, VGF subsidy, and contingent-liability payment through the PPP Risk Management Fund. Describe neutrally and accurately.
4. **H2**: "Vietnam: revenue sharing written into the PPP Law" — *covers*: Law No. 64/2020/QH14, ratified 18 June 2020, effective 1 January 2021; the codified symmetrical mechanism (above 125% of forecast revenue the State takes 50% of the excess; below 75% the State shares 50% of the shortfall); the foreign-exchange balance guarantee capped at 30% of project revenue in VND, subject to high-level approval. The symmetry is the interesting part — upside sharing as the price of downside protection.
5. **H2 (question format)**: "Which framework gives a sponsor the most usable comfort?" — *covers*: the honest answer that "most usable" depends on what the sponsor fears. **Deliver the article's mandatory table here**: country · statutory basis · who issues the comfort · form of support · how contingent liability is funded. India may appear as a fourth reference row via viability gap funding and model concession agreements, if the researcher can source it properly; otherwise leave it out rather than pad.
6. **H2 (honest / counter-conventional)**: "What a framework comparison cannot tell you" — *covers*: statutory generosity is not deal flow. A codified revenue guarantee that requires National Assembly approval may deliver less certainty than a smaller instrument issued routinely. What actually determines outcomes is project preparation quality, land acquisition, tariff-setting discipline, and demonstrated financial-close rates. Warn the reader explicitly against choosing a market from a comparison table — including this one. This is the trust-builder and the section that earns the article its credibility.

## PPP / guarantee topic to teach (vendor-neutral)

Teach how a national PPP framework is constructed: statute, institution, support instruments, contingent-liability management. Teach Indonesia's architecture accurately — a guarantee company operating as a single window with a ring-fenced balance sheet under the Ministry of Finance. Teach the Philippine and Vietnamese architectures with the same care and no comparative sneer. Teach that ring-fencing exists to protect the state budget as much as to reassure investors.
