# Public Private Partnership Step-In Rights: Do They Work?

*(56 characters. Primary keyword occupies the first 26 characters. The question ending is deliberate — it signals the honest answer the article delivers and separates it from every "what are step-in rights" definition page.)*

## Hook hypothesis

Step-in rights get negotiated harder than almost any clause in a PPP financing, often consuming weeks of legal time across the direct agreement. Then the evidence arrives: the Global Infrastructure Hub's PPP contract-management study found no substitution examples across 250 global projects. Lender step-in is designed to prevent termination, not to transfer a business — and in Indonesia there is a further wrinkle that offshore credit committees rarely price in. Step-in rights are not a recognised form of security under Indonesian positive law. They are built by conditional assignment, which is contractual rather than proprietary, and any substitute operator must clear government approval including nationality and track-record tests.

That combination — heavily negotiated, legally softer than assumed, and almost never exercised — is a genuinely useful thing to tell a lender before it spends the legal budget. No page in the top ten covers it. A&O Shearman, the strongest cross-border competitor, was fetched this run and covers step-in rights not at all.

PT PII's standing here is specific and limited: as the guarantee issuer it is a counterparty to the contractual architecture that step-in operates within, and it sees where remedies actually get used. The article must stay scrupulously inside that lane — the guarantee does not create, enforce or exercise step-in rights.

## Article skeleton — H2s the writer should expand

Each H2 opens with a 40–60-word direct answer before elaboration (GEO §2a).

1. **H2 (definitional)**: "What are step-in rights in a public private partnership?" — *covers*: the lender's contractual right to intervene in, or nominate a replacement for, the project company when default threatens the contract; that its purpose is to preserve the project and avert termination, not to acquire an asset.
2. **H2**: "The direct agreement: the document that makes step-in possible" — *covers*: the tripartite agreement between lenders, the project company and the contracting agency; the authority's undertaking not to terminate before lenders have had a chance to remedy; notice requirements. Explain why step-in without a direct agreement is close to worthless.
3. **H2**: "What actually happens when the project company defaults" — *covers*: the sequence, delivered as the article's **mandatory numbered list**: default event → notice to the contracting agency → standstill / cure period → lender intervention or nomination of a substitute → government approval of the substitute → hand-back or, failing all of it, termination and compensation. Keep this procedural and unhurried; it is the article's spine.
4. **H2 (question format)**: "Can a foreign lender legally take over an Indonesian project company?" — *covers*: the Indonesian-law reality. Step-in is not a recognised security interest; it is constructed by conditional assignment, contractual rather than proprietary. A substitute must satisfy government approval covering eligibility, nationality, track record, technical capability and financial standing. Sector foreign-ownership limits can constrain who qualifies. **Deliver the article's mandatory table here**: what lenders typically assume versus what Indonesian practice delivers.
5. **H2 (honest / counter-conventional)**: "Why step-in rights are negotiated hard and used almost never" — *covers*: the Global Infrastructure Hub finding of no substitution across 250 projects; that contractor insolvency (construction or O&M) is more common than project-company insolvency and is handled through performance bonds and parent guarantees rather than step-in; what a lender should negotiate instead — cure periods with realistic duration, clean notice mechanics, and a workable substitution consent standard. This is the trust-builder and the article's differentiator; give it real weight.

## PPP / guarantee topic to teach (vendor-neutral)

Teach the direct agreement and the step-in/substitution sequence accurately and neutrally. Teach the Indonesian legal characterisation honestly, including its limits. Teach the distinction between project-company insolvency and contractor insolvency, and which remedy addresses which. Make clear that the government guarantee and lender step-in are separate mechanisms that respond to different failures — the guarantee to government-side obligations, step-in to private-side performance.
