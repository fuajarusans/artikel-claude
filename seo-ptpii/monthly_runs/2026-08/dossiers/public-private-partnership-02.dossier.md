# Research Dossier: Public Private Partnership Bankability: Reaching Financial Close in Indonesia

## TL;DR for the writer

- **Major correction to the brief/knowledge base**: the implementing regulation cited for the government guarantee (PMK 260/PMK.011/2010 jo. PMK 8/PMK.08/2016) has been **revoked**, effective 18 October 2024, and replaced by **PMK 68/2024** ("Dukungan Pemerintah untuk Pembiayaan Infrastruktur melalui Skema Kerja Sama Pemerintah dan Badan Usaha dan/atau Skema Pembiayaan Lainnya"). The **same PMK 68/2024 also revoked and absorbed the old Viability Gap Fund regulation** (PMK 223/PMK.011/2012 jo. PMK 170/PMK.08/2018). Perpres 78/2010 and Perpres 38/2015 both remain in force at the presidential level — only the ministerial-level implementing rules changed. Do not cite PMK 260/2010, PMK 8/2016 or PMK 170/2018 as current law; cite PMK 68/2024.
- **The Fitch figures in the brief are CONFIRMED**: 'BBB' (Long-Term FC/LC IDR), 'F2' (Short-Term), 'AAA(idn)' (National Long-Term), Outlook Stable, affirmed **12 February 2026**; GRE support score 45/60; extraordinary support "Virtually Certain"; equity IDR 16.0 trillion; maximum guarantee capacity **IDR 191.6 trillion**; gearing 6.5x (2025) vs 6.1x (2024). This **corrects** `company_profile.md`'s stale "BBB-" entry — flag for knowledge-base update.
- **A conflicting capacity figure exists**: a 5 March 2026 media report attributes to PT PII management (unnamed per the blacklist) equity of ~Rp17.5 trillion, capacity of ~Rp115 trillion and gearing of "6.2–6.5x" — different numbers from the same general period as the Fitch action. Use the Fitch-sourced figures (more rigorous, dated, methodologically explicit) as primary; do not present both as equally current without a scope caveat.
- **The 12-month financial-close deadline is real but is practitioner-reported, not independently pulled from primary statutory text in this pass.** Frame it as market/practitioner practice unless the editor independently locates the operative article.
- **World Bank/MIGA co-guarantee is a nuanced, partly-historical claim.** The foundational co-guarantor structure (World Bank Group + Ministry of Finance backstop, a 2011 MIGA MOU) is well documented for IIGF's original design, but the most recent dated evidence (5 March 2026) has PT PII's own leadership describing cooperation with MIGA and the Islamic Development Bank's ICIEC as **still being explored**, not a concluded, active, quantified facility. Hedge accordingly.
- Two structurally different "funding gap" figures are both live in the market for the RPJMN 2025–2029 period — do not conflate them (see Core Facts #14–15 and Edge Cases).
- Two verified, approved-list, dated project examples are available and strong for this article: **SPAM Umbulan** (financial close 30 December 2016, six months ahead of schedule) and **Bogor–Serpong "Boser"** toll road (guarantee and regress agreements signed 3 October 2025, IDR 12.351 trillion project value).

## FAQ Q&As (drafted, English)

### How long does it take to reach financial close on an Indonesian PPP project?
Practitioner sources describe a working deadline of 12 months after signing the KPBU (PPP) agreement, extendable by up to six months where the delay is not the business entity's fault; missing that window can let the contracting agency terminate. Timing varies by project: SPAM Umbulan, a water-supply KPBU guaranteed by PT PII, reached financial close on 30 December 2016, six months ahead of its original target. Treat the 12-month figure as market practice, not confirmed statutory text, until independently verified.

### What is the difference between government support and a government guarantee in Indonesia?
Government support (Dukungan Pemerintah) — the Viability Gap Fund, Availability Payment, and project development facilities — makes a project financially viable by contributing cash or a payment stream. A government guarantee (Penjaminan Pemerintah), issued by PT PII as Indonesia's Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur, instead covers specific government-side risks under the contract, such as termination triggered by contracting-agency default. Since October 2024 both sit under one consolidated regulation, PMK 68/2024, but they solve different problems in a lender's financial model.

### Does the Viability Gap Fund apply to availability-payment projects?
No. Indonesia's Viability Gap Fund (Dukungan Kelayakan) is built for user-charge projects whose tariff revenue alone cannot make them financially viable; it contributes toward eligible construction-phase costs. Availability-payment projects are instead compensated through the contracted periodic payment itself, which lenders size debt against directly. A ceiling of up to 49% of eligible costs was reported under the now-superseded PMK 170/2018; confirm the current cap under PMK 68/2024 before quoting a percentage.

### Can a foreign lender finance an Indonesian PPP project?
Yes — foreign ownership is now permitted up to 100% in most infrastructure sectors — but foreign lenders remain a minority presence in the market. Practitioner commentary attributes this to structural factors rather than a lack of guarantees: counterparty risk that differs sharply between central-government, regional-government, and SOE obligors; reliance on domestic courts for disputes against a government counterpart; and sector regulation that can change well inside a 25–30-year concession term.

### What happens to lenders if the PPP contract is terminated early?
Lender protection sits mainly in the contract's termination compensation formula, not in the guarantee itself: a well-structured KPBU agreement sizes the termination payment to repay outstanding senior debt, and PT PII's guarantee can cover the government's own termination-payment obligation where the trigger is a covered government-side risk. Lenders diligence the formula's mechanics closely — deductions, valuation basis, timing — because a poorly drafted formula can leave debt short even where a guarantee exists.

### Is the government guarantee provider itself rated?
Yes. Fitch Ratings affirmed PT Penjaminan Infrastruktur Indonesia (PT PII), internationally known as the Indonesia Infrastructure Guarantee Fund (IIGF), at Long-Term Foreign- and Local-Currency IDR 'BBB', Short-Term 'F2', and National Long-Term 'AAA(idn)', Outlook Stable, on 12 February 2026. The rating is credit-linked to Indonesia's sovereign rating (also BBB/Stable) because PT PII is a Government-Related Entity wholly owned through the Ministry of Finance — so a lender can assess the guarantor's standing directly rather than take it on faith.

### How does a public-private partnership work in Indonesia?
Indonesia's PPP scheme is called KPBU (Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha), governed primarily by Perpres 38/2015. A government contracting agency (PJPK) contracts a private business entity to design, finance, build, and operate public infrastructure over a long-term concession, recovering its investment through user charges, an availability payment, or a mix of both. The government may layer on support (viability funding) or a guarantee, issued by PT PII, to help the project reach a bankable credit profile.

### What is a PPP project?
A public-private partnership (PPP) project is a long-term arrangement in which a government body contracts a private company to finance, build, operate, or maintain public infrastructure — a toll road, water treatment plant, or power facility, for example — in exchange for a revenue stream defined in the contract, whether user charges, government payments, or both. In Indonesia, this structure is formally named KPBU and governed by Perpres 38/2015.

### What a lender diligences on an Indonesian PPP

| Diligence item | What the lender is testing | Where it usually breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue mechanism | User-charge demand risk vs. contracted availability payment | Demand-risk assumptions on a user-charge project; late clarity on which mechanism applies |
| Payment obligor identity | Whether the obligor is central government, regional government, an SOE, or a regional-owned company | Regional/SOE obligors carry materially different counterparty risk than the central government |
| Termination compensation formula | Whether the payment on early termination repays outstanding senior debt | Formula drafting — deductions, valuation basis, timing of payment |
| Step-in rights / lender direct agreement | Whether lenders can intervene and preserve the project if it is in distress | Step-in is contract-based in Indonesia, not a freestanding statutory right — must be negotiated project by project |
| Security package and perfection | Enforceability of pledges/assignments under Indonesian law | Perfection mechanics and local-court enforcement against a government counterparty |
| Currency of revenue vs. currency of debt | IDR-denominated revenue against hard-currency debt service | Convertibility is generally unrestricted, but IDR cannot be transferred offshore by Indonesian banks under any circumstance — a structural mismatch point |
| Guarantee cap, tenor and fee | What the guarantee actually covers, its ceiling, and its duration relative to the debt tenor | Exposure above the cap is uncovered; guarantee tenor (~15 years, indicative) vs. concession/debt tenor mismatch |
| Insurance and force majeure | Standard project-finance insurance package and force majeure allocation | Sector- and site-specific — no single Indonesia-wide answer |

### Government Support vs. Government Guarantee

| Aspect | Government Support (Dukungan Pemerintah) | Government Guarantee (Penjaminan Pemerintah) |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Contributes cash or a payment stream to make a project financially viable | Covers specific government-side risks under the KPBU contract |
| Instruments | Viability Gap Fund (Dukungan Kelayakan), Availability Payment, Pra-PDF/PDF project-development facilities | Guarantee issued by PT PII as BUPI, paired with a regress agreement |
| Who it applies to | VGF: user-charge projects only. Availability Payment: projects paid on service availability, not user demand | Any KPBU project where the government carries a defined contractual risk |
| Legal basis (current) | PMK 68/2024 (replacing PMK 223/2012 jo. PMK 170/2018) | Perpres 78/2010 (umbrella) + PMK 68/2024 (implementing, replacing PMK 260/2010 jo. PMK 8/2016) |
| What it is not | Not a guarantee against government default | Not a subsidy; does not remove the business entity's commercial risk |

## Edge cases & nuance

- **A Perpres can stay in force while its implementing PMK is replaced.** Perpres 78/2010 and Perpres 38/2015 are both still "Berlaku." Only the Menteri Keuangan-level implementing rules (PMK 260/2010 jo. PMK 8/2016, and PMK 223/2012 jo. PMK 170/2018) were revoked and consolidated into PMK 68/2024 in October 2024. An article that cites "Perpres 78/2010 jo. PMK 260/2010" as one unbroken, currently-operative chain is now technically wrong at the PMK link even though it is right at the Perpres link.
- **Two funding-gap figures are both in circulation for RPJMN 2025–2029 and must never be blended**: (A) IDR 1,905.3 trillion total need / IDR 753.11 trillion gap (~USD 46bn), widely reported June 2025, appears to be the APBN/APBD-referenced planning figure; (B) Bappenas's PPP Book 2025 reportedly frames total infrastructure "stock" need at roughly IDR 10,303 trillion with a much larger gap (~IDR 6,182 trillion). These answer different questions — state one, name its scope, and do not average them.
- **Regress does not make the guarantee less certain to the lender/beneficiary.** The regress right lets PT PII, after paying a valid claim, seek reimbursement from the government party actually responsible. It reallocates the fiscal burden after payment; it does not delay or condition the payment itself. This is the ring-fencing mechanism at work, not a loophole a lender should worry about — but it is also why the guarantee is not "free money" to the contracting agency, and why PT PII's own claims history and capacity headroom (the Fitch figures) matter to a credit analyst assessing the guarantor.
- **A guarantee reaches only enumerated government-side risks.** It does not cover demand risk, does not cap construction cost overruns, and will not compensate for a termination-compensation formula that was drafted to leave senior debt short. "Bankable" is a property of the whole package — revenue mechanism, contract drafting, and counterparty standing together — not something a guarantee alone confers on a weak project.
- **Step-in rights and termination-payment mechanics are contract-based in Indonesia**, not a freestanding statutory entitlement the way some other jurisdictions structure PPP law. An offshore lender's counsel needs to check the specific KPBU agreement's drafting and the lender direct agreement, not assume a statutory default position.
- **Government Support and Government Guarantee are frequently and incorrectly used interchangeably** in secondary sources and even in some SERP-ranking pages (this is the gap the brief specifically flags). Keep them structurally distinct throughout the article; the comparison table above is built for this purpose.
- **Foreign-participation figures are dated.** The ADB PPP Monitor's "72 of 135 projects with foreign sponsor participation" is 2019-vintage; it is a reasonable historical anchor but should never be presented as describing today's market share.

## Definition snippet draft (≈56 words)

> Bankability in a public private partnership means lenders judge the project's contracted cash flow, risk allocation, and counterparty standing strong enough to support project-finance debt. In Indonesia's PPP scheme, known domestically as KPBU (Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha), bankability usually turns on the revenue mechanism, the termination compensation formula, and who stands behind the government's own contractual obligations.
