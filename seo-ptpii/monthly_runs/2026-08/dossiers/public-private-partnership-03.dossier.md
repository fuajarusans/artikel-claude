# Research Dossier: Public Private Partnership Guarantees: Indonesia's Cover vs MIGA and MDBs

## TL;DR for the writer

- **The stacking answer is three-part, not a single yes/no — get this exactly right.** (1) A domestic co-guarantee between the Ministry of Finance and PT PII (*Penjaminan Bersama*) is a **confirmed, legally defined, currently operating** mechanism, activated when a project's guarantee need exceeds PT PII's own capacity. (2) PT PII has **historically** operated alongside a World Bank Group guarantee as co-guarantor on **specific named flagship projects** (documented World Bank project archive, dated). (3) A standing MIGA co-guarantee mechanism is **not yet operational** — PT PII's own 4 February 2025 press release describes the relationship as "*penjajakan potensi kolaborasi strategis*" (exploring potential strategic collaboration), and March 2026 coverage still describes PT PII as "seeking" (*mengincar*) international partners including the World Bank and IsDB. Write this as a hedged, dated answer, not a confident "yes, it stacks."
- **Major regulatory correction to the brief's assumed legal basis.** PMK No. 260/PMK.011/2010 jo. PMK No. 8/PMK.08/2016 — the implementing regulation for infrastructure guarantees — was **revoked effective 18 October 2024** and replaced by **PMK No. 68/2024** (Dukungan Pemerintah untuk Pembiayaan Infrastruktur melalui Skema Kerja Sama Pemerintah dan Badan Usaha dan/atau Skema Pembiayaan Lainnya). **Perpres No. 78 Tahun 2010 itself remains in force** as the presidential-level legal basis; only its Ministry of Finance implementing regulation changed. Cite PMK 68/2024, not the old PMK 260/2010, as the current implementing instrument.
- **Portfolio and ratings are both confirmed and current.** Portfolio: **61 projects (38 KPBU + 23 non-KPBU), 7 sectors, total value >IDR 732 trillion, as at June 2026** (`ptpii.co.id/informasi-proyek`, fetched 2026-08-12) — the English hub's "33 projects" is confirmed stale; do not use it. Rating: Fitch affirmed **'BBB' (Long-Term Foreign- and Local-Currency IDR) / 'F2' (Short-Term) / 'AAA(idn)' (National Long-Term), Outlook Stable, on 12 February 2026**, with maximum guarantee capacity **IDR 191.6 trillion on equity of IDR 16.0 trillion** (as of end-December 2025). `company_profile.md` as currently written already reflects 'BBB' correctly — the brief's warning about a stale 'BBB-' appears already resolved in the knowledge base.
- **MIGA's current product architecture is organised into three families**, not a single flat list: Political Risk Guarantees (currency inconvertibility and transfer restriction, expropriation, war and civil disturbance, breach of contract), Credit Guarantees (including non-honouring of public debt/financial obligations), and Trade Finance Guarantees — all now delivered through the **World Bank Group Guarantee Platform**, live since **1 July 2024**, which consolidated IBRD/IDA/IFC/MIGA guarantee products under one roof at MIGA. Use this current framing rather than treating "MIGA" and "the World Bank" as separate silos.
- **Whether PT PII's guarantee covers currency inconvertibility is genuinely contested between sources** — PT PII's own English hub and its published risk list do not mention it; one secondary aggregator (ADB PPP Monitor, 2019-vintage) lists a broader risk universe that includes it. Do not assert either way; use "not published" framing.

One claim per cell; consistent column order for citation extraction.

| Instrument | Provider type | Risks covered | Who is the beneficiary | Claim trigger & mechanics | Recourse / subrogation | Can it stack? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Indonesia PPP government guarantee** (issued by PT PII / BUPI) | State-owned guarantee company (Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur), Special Mission Vehicle of the Ministry of Finance — not a lender or insurer | Government-side contract risk: discriminatory project-specific change of law; delayed/failed government approvals; early termination on government/PJPK default; other government acts or omissions under the KPBU contract | The business entity (project company/SPV) party to the KPBU cooperation agreement | PJPK's proven default under a bilateral Guarantee Agreement tied to the underlying KPBU contract | *Regress* right — PT PII reclaims a paid claim from the responsible PJPK via a separate Regress Agreement | Yes domestically, via *Penjaminan Bersama* with the Ministry of Finance (confirmed, PMK 68/2024); layering with MIGA/MDB cover is deal-specific and not yet a standing arrangement |

| **Export Credit Agency (ECA) cover** | A national export credit agency, official or officially supported, tied to its own country's exports | Commercial risk (buyer/borrower default or insolvency) and political risk (non-payment from host-government action); exact terms are agency-specific | The exporter, or the lender financing the export/project contract | Varies by agency and by support type (direct financing vs. cover on private lending); participating agencies coordinate under the OECD Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits | Agency-specific recovery mechanisms after a paid claim | Yes in principle — commonly layered alongside other guarantees/insurance on the same project financing, subject to each instrument's own eligibility rules |

**Typical use case, one line each** (for prose around the table, not the table itself):
- Indonesia's guarantee: a foreign or domestic sponsor needs certainty that the PJPK's own contractual promises — an approval, a tariff adjustment, a termination payment — will be honoured.
- MIGA: a foreign equity investor or lender wants cover against sovereign-level events (expropriation, war, currency transfer restriction) that sit above and beyond any single contract.
- MDB partial risk/credit guarantee: a commercial lender is willing to take the project's credit risk but not its political risk, or wants a credit enhancement to reach a lower cost of funds.
- ECA cover: a transaction involves exported equipment, works, or services, and the sponsor's or lender's own export credit agency has an interest in supporting that trade.

## FAQ Q&As (English, 8 drafted)

### What is the difference between a government guarantee and political risk insurance?
A government guarantee, as issued by PT Penjaminan Infrastruktur Indonesia (PT PII/IIGF), covers the government contracting agency's own contractual obligations to the project company under a KPBU/PPP agreement — for example, a blocked approval or an unpaid termination payment. Political risk insurance, MIGA's product, instead insures the foreign investor or lender directly against sovereign-level events, and typically pays only after a waiting period and, for contract disputes, an arbitral award (miga.org, accessed 2026-08-12).

### Does Indonesia's PPP guarantee cover currency inconvertibility?
PT PII's published guaranteed-risk categories — discriminatory change of law, delayed or failed government approvals, early termination on government default, and other government acts or omissions — do not name currency inconvertibility, and PT PII's own English hub does not mention it either (ptpii.co.id, 2026). Some third-party comparative sources describe a broader risk universe for Indonesia's guarantee, so this remains unconfirmed rather than settled either way. MIGA's own product, by contrast, explicitly covers currency inconvertibility and transfer restriction.

### Is the Indonesian guarantee provider rated?
Yes. Fitch Ratings affirmed PT PII's Long-Term Foreign- and Local-Currency Issuer Default Ratings at 'BBB', Short-Term Foreign-Currency IDR at 'F2', and National Long-Term Rating at 'AAA(idn)', Outlook Stable, on 12 February 2026. The rating is equalised with Indonesia's sovereign rating because Fitch classifies PT PII as a government-related entity wholly owned through the Ministry of Finance (Fitch Ratings, 12 February 2026).

### Can MIGA cover and an Indonesian government guarantee apply to the same project?
In principle yes, since the two instruments typically protect different counterparties — PT PII's guarantee runs to the local project company against the government's own default, while MIGA insures the foreign investor or lender directly. Indonesia does operate a confirmed domestic co-guarantee mechanism between the Ministry of Finance and PT PII (PMK 68/2024), and PT PII has previously worked alongside the World Bank Group on specific projects. A standing arrangement with MIGA specifically was still described as exploratory collaboration in reporting through early 2026, so verify current status deal by deal.

### Is PPP the same as KPBU?
Yes. KPBU (*Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha*) is Indonesia's domestic legal name for what international practice calls a public-private partnership, governed by Peraturan Presiden No. 38 Tahun 2015. It defines the cooperation scheme between government and business entities to provide public infrastructure, with financing and risk shared between them. International sponsors will see "KPBU" in Indonesian contracts and regulations describing the same underlying scheme they know as PPP.

### Who pays a claim under an Indonesian PPP guarantee, and can it be recovered?
PT PII pays the business entity directly once a covered government-side default under the KPBU contract is established, under a bilateral Guarantee Agreement. This is not automatically a final cost to the state: PT PII holds a *regress* right, formalised in a separate Regress Agreement with the government contracting agency (PJPK), allowing PT PII to reclaim the amount it paid from the party actually responsible for the default.

### What is a partial risk guarantee?
A partial risk guarantee is a bank-type instrument, issued by a multilateral development bank such as the World Bank Group or the Asian Development Bank, that covers a lender against defined political or government-related risks under a project agreement — for instance, a state utility's failure to make a termination payment. Unlike insurance-type political risk cover, it is generally structured to pay once its trigger conditions are met, without a mandatory waiting period or arbitration step (Asian Development Bank; World Bank Group Guarantee Platform, live since July 2024).

### Can you give me an example of a public-private partnership project in Indonesia?
PT PII has guaranteed KPBU projects across sectors including toll roads, drinking-water supply and telecommunications — for example the Palapa Ring fibre-optic backbone and toll-road concessions such as Kediri–Tulungagung. As at June 2026, PT PII's cumulative portfolio spans 61 guaranteed projects (38 KPBU and 23 non-KPBU) across seven sectors, with a combined project value exceeding IDR 732 trillion (ptpii.co.id, June 2026).

## Edge cases & nuance

- **Two different meanings of "stacking" are in play, and the article must not conflate them.** Domestic co-guarantee (*Penjaminan Bersama* between the Ministry of Finance and PT PII) stacks two Indonesian government-side guarantee providers on the *same* risk. Layering PT PII's guarantee with MIGA or an MDB instrument stacks *different* instruments protecting *different* counterparties (the local project company vs. the foreign investor/lender) — structurally compatible, but not the same thing as the domestic mechanism, and not yet evidenced as a standing operational pairing with MIGA specifically.
- **Penjaminan pemerintah ≠ subsidy, and it does not remove the business entity's commercial risk.** It covers government-side risk under the contract only — demand risk, construction-cost overruns, and operating-cost risk remain with the business entity regardless of any guarantee in place.
- **The regress right is the reason the guarantee is not a one-way fiscal transfer.** A paid claim is, in principle, recoverable from the party responsible (typically the PJPK) — this is the mechanism that lets ring-fencing coexist with the guarantee actually paying out.
- **"Non-honouring of financial obligations" is not, on MIGA's current site architecture, filed under the same product family as the classic political risk list.** It sits under MIGA's Credit Guarantees family ("Non-honoring of Public Debt") rather than the core Political Risk Guarantees family that holds currency inconvertibility, expropriation, war/civil disturbance and breach of contract. Older comparative literature often lists all five together as "MIGA's covered risks," which remains broadly accurate in substance but is not how MIGA currently organises its own product menu.
- **The 2024 World Bank Group Guarantee Platform changes the comparison's vocabulary.** Before July 2024, commentary typically discussed "IBRD guarantees" and "MIGA insurance" as separate offerings. They are now marketed as one consolidated menu at MIGA. An article written as though these remain separate institutional silos will read as dated to an informed reader.
- **A Kemenkeu explainer page can lag behind the primary regulation database.** The KPBU portal's own "Penjaminan Infrastruktur" page still cites PMK 260/2010 and PMK 8/2016 as legal basis, even though the JDIH regulation database confirms both were superseded by PMK 68/2024 in October 2024. When a topic explainer and a regulation registry disagree on citation, the registry is the higher authority for legal status.
- **Guarantee capacity is finite and priced, on every instrument compared here** — none of the four is unlimited or free; each carries caps, fees, and (for PT PII, per secondary-source commentary) a tenor limit of roughly 15 years. This is a fair, symmetric point to make about all four instruments, not a weakness unique to Indonesia's.
