# Research Dossier: Risk Allocation in Indonesia's Public Private Partnership: Who Carries What

## TL;DR for the writer

- **Headline correction — use this, it resolves two of the brief's open questions at once.** The old legal-basis pairing "Perpres 78/2010 + PMK 260/PMK.011/2010 jo. PMK 8/PMK.08/2016" is **half current**. Perpres 78/2010 (the guarantee framework establishing BUPI) is still in force. But **PMK 260/PMK.011/2010 has been superseded** by **Peraturan Menteri Keuangan (PMK) No. 68 Tahun 2024** (Minister of Finance Regulation No. 68 of 2024, on government support for infrastructure financing through PPP and other financing schemes), issued 2024, currently in force. Do not cite PMK 260/2010 jo. PMK 8/2016 as live law. Cite **Perpres 78/2010 (framework) + PMK 68/2024 (current implementing regulation)**.
- **PMK 68/2024's Pasal 75 (Article 75) mandates PT PII, as BUPI, to publish an annual "Acuan Alokasi Risiko" (Risk Allocation Guideline/Reference)** — this is CONFIRMED, well-corroborated. What is **NOT confirmed** is the strategist's claim of "11 risk categories": the 2025 PDF is too large to fetch directly (exceeds tool limits), and the only structure independently observable is that PT PII publishes the guideline **by sector** (at least Jalan/Roads, Olahraga/Sports, Kesehatan/Health, Telekomunikasi, Kawasan, Air Minum are listed), not as a flat 11-item list. Do not state "11 categories" in the article. Use the four verified guaranteed risk families instead (below).
- **PT PII's guarantee reaches four risk families**, all government-side: (1) discriminatory, project-specific change of law; (2) delay/failure to obtain key government approvals; (3) early termination on PJPK (government) default; (4) other acts/omissions of government as contract counterparty. In market practice this is described as covering **termination payments, delayed tariff adjustment, and delayed land procurement**, typically capped, with a tenor around **15 years** and upfront-plus-recurring fees — but that cap/tenor/fee detail is **law-firm-sourced, not from a PT PII first-party publication**; attribute it accordingly.
- **The "no" rows are the article's real payload**: demand/traffic/volume risk, construction cost overrun, operating cost, and FX/IDR-revenue-denomination risk are **not** reached by the guarantee — they stay with the Badan Usaha Pelaksana (project company) or its lenders. This is explicit, sourced, and safe to assert plainly.
- **Portfolio, dated correctly**: as at **June 2026**, PT PII had guaranteed **61 projects (38 KPBU + 23 non-KPBU)** worth **more than Rp 732 trillion**, confirmed by direct fetch of `ptpii.co.id/informasi-proyek`. That page also states "7 sectors" while naming eight (Jalan, Ketenagalistrikan, Telekomunikasi, Air Minum, Pariwisata, Transportasi, Persampahan, Konservasi Energi) — a real inconsistency on PT PII's own site; do not reproduce it, and do not use the English hub's stale "33 projects" figure.
- **Fitch affirmed PT PII at BBB (Long-Term FC/LC IDR) / F2 (Short-Term FC) / AAA(idn) (National Long-Term), Outlook Stable, on 12 February 2026**, with a maximum guarantee capacity of **Rp 191.6 trillion on Rp 16.0 trillion equity** (gearing 6.5x in 2025 against a 12x ceiling — the two figures are internally consistent). This is a strong, dated, well-sourced counterparty-credit fact for a lender/DFI audience.
- **Never conflate the two funding-gap figures in circulation**: (a) RPJMN 2025–2029's own planning figure — Rp 1,905.3 trillion total need, Rp 753.11 trillion gap (39.5%), reported mid-2025; versus (b) a much larger, later, differently-scoped figure — Rp 10,303 trillion needed to hit the government's 8% GDP growth target, reported December 2025 by a coordinating ministry. State scope, issuing body, and date every time either appears.

## Core facts (verified)

### A. Legal and regulatory framework

> **Claim**: The core Indonesian PPP (KPBU) legal framework regulation is in force and correctly numbered.
> **Number/specific**: Peraturan Presiden (Perpres) No. 38 Tahun 2015 tentang Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha dalam Penyediaan Infrastruktur (Presidential Regulation No. 38 of 2015 on Government Cooperation with Business Entities in Infrastructure Provision) — effective 20 March 2015, status **Berlaku** (in force) as at the access date, no later amendment or revocation found. It revoked the prior chain beginning with Perpres 67/2005.
> **Source**: JDIH Kementerian Keuangan, `https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/perpres-38-tahun-2015`, accessed 2026-08-12; corroborated by `https://peraturan.go.id/id/perpres-no-38-tahun-2015`.
> **Confidence**: high

> **Claim**: The Presidential-level regulation establishing infrastructure guarantees through a Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur (BUPI) is in force.
> **Number/specific**: Perpres No. 78 Tahun 2010 tentang Penjaminan Infrastruktur dalam Proyek Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha yang Dilakukan Melalui Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur — effective 21 December 2010, status **Berlaku**, no recorded amendment/revocation ("Tidak ada riwayat").
> **Source**: JDIH Kementerian Keuangan, `https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/perpres-78-tahun-2010`, accessed 2026-08-12.
> **Confidence**: high

> **Claim**: The 2010-era ministerial implementing regulation for infrastructure guarantees is **no longer current law**.
> **Number/specific**: PMK No. 260/PMK.011/2010 (Petunjuk Pelaksanaan Penjaminan Infrastruktur, as amended by PMK No. 8/PMK.08/2016) has been **superseded by PMK No. 68 Tahun 2024**, issued 2024, which explicitly revokes PMK 260/PMK.011/2010 among six-to-seven prior Kemenkeu regulations on government support/guarantee for infrastructure financing.
> **Source**: JDIH Kemenkeu summary of PMK 68/2024, `https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/pmk-68-tahun-2024`, and corroborating search results naming PMK 260/PMK.011/2010 in the revoked list, accessed 2026-08-12.
> **Confidence**: medium-high (the consolidated revocation list is corroborated across independent sources; the precise textual treatment of PMK 8/PMK.08/2016 specifically — an amendment to the now-revoked 260/2010 — could not be read verbatim in the statute's closing articles. Note also that the official Kemenkeu KPBU portal page, `kpbu.kemenkeu.go.id`, still lists Perpres 78/2010 + PMK 260/2010 + PMK 8/2016 as "legal basis" for penjaminan infrastruktur without reflecting PMK 68/2024 — treat that specific page as stale; prefer JDIH.)

> **Claim**: PMK 68/2024 is the current, consolidated Kemenkeu regulation governing both government support and government guarantee implementation for KPBU, and it is the legal basis for PT PII's annual risk-allocation reference book.
> **Number/specific**: PMK No. 68 Tahun 2024 tentang Dukungan Pemerintah untuk Pembiayaan Infrastruktur melalui Skema Kerja Sama Pemerintah dan Badan Usaha dan/atau Skema Pembiayaan Lainnya (2024). **Pasal 75** mandates publication of an "Acuan Alokasi Risiko" (risk-category/distribution reference between public and private sectors) as the primary reference for PJPK drafting KPBU agreements, submitting guarantee proposals to PT PII, and for lenders/investors pricing KPBU deals.
> **Source**: JDIH Kemenkeu, `https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/pmk-68-tahun-2024`; corroborating description matched independently across `kpbu.kemenkeu.go.id` search indexing and PT PII's own book-description text, accessed 2026-08-12.
> **Confidence**: medium-high (specific, consistent, cross-corroborated description; raw statutory text of Pasal 75 itself not read directly due to fetch access limits — see Flags)

> **Claim**: The Viability Gap Fund's construction-cost ceiling now sits under PMK 68/2024, not the older PMK cited in earlier secondary sources.
> **Number/specific**: Dukungan Kelayakan (Viability Gap Fund) may cover up to **49% of construction, equipment and installation cost**, cash-based, for projects that are economically viable but not yet financially viable. The regulation historically cited for this (PMK 223/PMK.011/2012, PMK 143/PMK.011/2013, PMK 170/PMK.08/2018) has been **folded into PMK 68 Tahun 2024** (2024), the current basis.
> **Source**: Search-corroborated description of PMK 68/2024's VGF provisions plus its revocation of PMK 170/PMK.08/2018, PMK 223/PMK.011/2012 and PMK 143/PMK.011/2013, accessed 2026-08-12; cross-referenced against DJPPR KPBU program description.
> **Confidence**: medium-high (the 49% figure itself is long-standing and widely cited; the point being verified here — that its current legal basis is PMK 68/2024, not the older PMKs — is corroborated but not read in the primary statutory text directly)

> **Claim**: Bappenas's KPBU implementation regulation, and its most recent amendment, are correctly identified and dated.
> **Number/specific**: Peraturan Menteri PPN/Kepala Bappenas No. 7 Tahun 2023 tentang Pelaksanaan Kerja Sama Pemerintah dan Badan Usaha dalam Penyediaan Infrastruktur is the base KPBU-process regulation. It was **amended by Peraturan Menteri PPN/Kepala Bappenas No. 9 Tahun 2025**, effective **30 December 2025**, which revises general provisions, the economic/social infrastructure classification, delegation of authority, the Panitia Pengadaan Badan Usaha Pelaksana (procurement committee), Badan Penyiapan (project preparation body), the KPBU process, the KPBU agreement, winner determination, and simplified KPBU cooperation — a **procedural/process update, not a new risk-allocation instrument**.
> **Source**: `https://peraturan.go.id/id/permen-ppn-bappenas-no-9-tahun-2025` and `https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/341427/permen-ppnkepala-bappenas-no-9-tahun-2025`, accessed 2026-08-12 — both primary regulatory databases, independent of the paywalled/403 law-firm commentary the brief flagged as unread.
> **Confidence**: high (this directly resolves the brief's open question about "Bappenas Regulation 9/2025" without relying on the inaccessible Dentons article)

> **Claim**: Foreign ownership in most Indonesian infrastructure sectors can reach 100%, under a regulation that is confirmed in force.
> **Number/specific**: Peraturan Presiden No. 10 Tahun 2021 tentang Bidang Usaha Penanaman Modal (2021) — status **Berlaku** — replaced the prior closed/restricted business-field list regime (Perpres 44/2016, itself replacing Perpres 76/2007). Law-firm commentary describes the resulting reform as permitting **up to 100% foreign investment in most infrastructure sectors**.
> **Source**: `https://peraturan.go.id/id/perpres-no-10-tahun-2021`, accessed 2026-08-12, for the regulation's status; A&O Shearman, "Infrastructure series: Public Private Partnerships (PPP) in Indonesia," `https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/infrastructure-series-public-private-partnerships-ppp-in-indonesia`, accessed 2026-08-12, for the 100% figure.
> **Confidence**: medium-high (regulation status confirmed directly; the specific "100% in most sectors" figure is law-firm sourced and not independently cross-checked against a sector-by-sector list)

### B. What the guarantee covers, and what it does not

> **Claim**: PT PII's government guarantee is limited to four defined, government-side risk families.
> **Number/specific**: (1) discriminatory, project-specific change of law; (2) delay or failure to obtain key government approvals; (3) early termination triggered by PJPK (government) default; (4) other acts or omissions of government as a party to the KPBU contract. PT PII guarantees across **three project stages**: pre-construction, construction, and operation.
> **Source**: PT PII company profile (pre-approved, `knowledge_base/company_profile.md`), cross-verified in substance against Kementerian Keuangan's own KPBU guarantee page, `https://kpbu.kemenkeu.go.id/read/64-64/pjpk/dukungan-pemerintah/penjaminan-infrastruktur`, accessed 2026-08-12 (legal basis section: Perpres 78/2010, PMK 260/2010, PMK 8/2016 — see the "stale page" flag above), and against A&O Shearman's independent description below.
> **Confidence**: high

> **Claim**: In market practice, the guarantee's reach is described as covering specific payment triggers, with a cap, a tenor, and fees.
> **Number/specific**: IIGF/PT PII's guarantee is reported to cover "**termination payments due to political risks, delay in tariff adjustment and delay in land procurement**"; it is **typically capped** (amounts beyond the cap are not guaranteed); the **term is normally up to 15 years**; **upfront and recurring (six-monthly) fees** are payable to PT PII.
> **Source**: A&O Shearman, "Infrastructure series: Public Private Partnerships (PPP) in Indonesia," `https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/infrastructure-series-public-private-partnerships-ppp-in-indonesia`, accessed 2026-08-12 (direct fetch, quotes captured verbatim).
> **Confidence**: medium (credible international law firm, but this is **not** sourced to a PT PII first-party publication with the same cap/tenor/fee specificity — attribute to "market practice" or "as described by transaction advisers," not to PT PII directly, unless a first-party source is later found)

> **Claim**: The guarantee explicitly excludes the project company's own commercial risk and is not a subsidy or revenue floor.
> **Number/specific**: Penjaminan pemerintah ≠ subsidi; it does not remove the Badan Usaha Pelaksana's commercial risk. Demand/traffic/volume risk, construction cost overrun, operating cost risk, and FX/currency risk are **not** among the four guaranteed families and are not described as guarantee triggers in any source reviewed.
> **Source**: `knowledge_base/company_profile.md` (pre-approved nuance line); consistent with the absence of "demand," "construction," "operating cost," or "FX" from both the company-profile risk list and the A&O Shearman coverage description above.
> **Confidence**: high

> **Claim**: A PT PII guarantee payout is recoverable from the government party actually responsible — it is not a final, absorbed state cost.
> **Number/specific**: PT PII structures each guarantee under a **Guarantee and Regress Agreement** (Perjanjian Penjaminan dan Regres), giving it the right to reclaim, from the responsible party, amounts already paid out on a claim.
> **Source**: `knowledge_base/company_profile.md` ("hak regress"). Note: specific named toll-road examples reported in prior research could not be independently re-verified this session — see Flags; do not name unverified projects.
> **Confidence**: high (mechanism); the two specific named examples are unverified — do not use them

> **Claim**: For projects beyond PT PII's own capacity, the state (and multilateral partners) can back the guarantee jointly.
> **Number/specific**: PT PII's guarantees are described as "backed up by co-guarantors, including the Ministry of Finance and the World Bank Group" — a co-guarantee structure for larger projects, with PT PII administering the joint guarantee.
> **Source**: Global Infrastructure Hub case study, `https://www.gihub.org/emerging-funding-and-finance/case-studies/promote-private-investment-in-infrastructure-projects-by-providing-government-guarantees/`, accessed 2026-08-12 (direct fetch).
> **Confidence**: high (third-party institutional source), though the case study's own worked figures (21 PPP projects, Rp 210 trillion / USD 14.6 billion, 2 direct-loan guarantees worth Rp 6 trillion / USD 416 million) are **explicitly a 2010–2020 historical window** — do not present as current.

> **Number/specific**: ADB's PPP Monitor references "**Risk Allocation Guideline: Public Private Partnership (PPP) in Indonesia**," a **2018** IIGF/PT PII publication — an earlier edition of the same instrument later put on an annual statutory footing by PMK 68/2024 (2024) and now issued as the "Acuan Alokasi Risiko," most recently a 2025 edition.
> **Source**: Direct fetch of `https://pppmonitor.adb.org/country/indonesia/national-ppp-landscape`, accessed 2026-08-12.

> **Claim**: MIGA's political risk insurance — the standard non-Indonesian comparison instrument for currency and political risk — has a defined, different coverage list than PT PII's guarantee.
> **Number/specific**: MIGA political risk guarantees cover **Breach of Contract**, **Currency Inconvertibility and Transfer Restriction**, **War and Civil Disturbance**, and **Expropriation**; separate MIGA credit guarantees cover **Non-Honoring of Public Debt/Sovereign Financial Obligations**.

> **Claim**: Foreign sponsors flag three specific, named deterrents to Indonesian PPP participation, independent of the risk-allocation matrix itself.
> **Number/specific**: (1) **Counterparty risk** — distinguishing central government, regional government, SOEs, and regional-owned companies, compounded by IDR-denominated revenue against foreign-currency return expectations; (2) **Enforcement risk** — "bringing a dispute against a government counterpart in the local courts is likely to be unconvincing"; (3) **Regulatory risk** — sectoral regulation "often short-lived" against 25–30-year concessions.
> **Source**: A&O Shearman, `https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/infrastructure-series-public-private-partnerships-ppp-in-indonesia`, accessed 2026-08-12 (direct fetch, quotes captured verbatim).
> **Confidence**: high (direct quotes from a credible international law firm's own published analysis)

| What to teach (KPBU / penjaminan / project finance) | Facts to state accurately | Where in article | PT PII product to anchor the CTA |
|---|---|---|---|

## FAQ Q&As (8 drafted, English)

### How does a public-private partnership work in Indonesia?
In Indonesia, a public-private partnership — domestically called KPBU (Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha) — is a long-term contract between a government contracting agency (PJPK) and a private project company (Badan Usaha Pelaksana) to finance, build, and operate public infrastructure. The framework sits under Peraturan Presiden No. 38 Tahun 2015. Each risk is assigned contractually to whichever party can best control it, and government-side risk can be covered by a state guarantee issued through PT Penjaminan Infrastruktur Indonesia (PT PII).

### Is PPP the same as KPBU?
Yes, in substance. KPBU is Indonesia's domestic legal term for what international practice calls a public-private partnership, defined under Peraturan Presiden No. 38 Tahun 2015. Both describe the same structure: a government agency partnering with a private business entity to deliver public infrastructure under a long-term contract. KPBU does carry Indonesia-specific procedural requirements — project stages, procurement rules, and risk-allocation guidance set by Bappenas and the Ministry of Finance — that a generic international PPP reference would not automatically include.

### What risks does the Indonesian government guarantee cover?
PT PII's government guarantee reaches risks the state is contractually responsible for: project-specific discriminatory changes in law, delay or failure by government to issue required approvals, early contract termination triggered by government (PJPK) default, and other acts or omissions of government as a contracting party. As at June 2026, PT PII had guaranteed 61 infrastructure projects — 38 KPBU and 23 non-KPBU — across seven sectors. It does not cover the project company's own commercial or operating risk.

### Does a government guarantee cover demand or traffic risk?
No. Demand, traffic, and volume risk are treated as commercial risk in Indonesia's PPP framework and stay with the project company and its lenders — PT PII's guarantee does not reach them. The allocation is deliberate: the private sponsor is considered better placed to forecast and manage market risk than the state is. Where a project needs protection from revenue volatility, the tool typically used is a different government-support instrument, such as an availability payment, not a guarantee claim.

### What is the difference between government support and a government guarantee in Indonesia?
Government support (dukungan pemerintah) — such as the Viability Gap Fund, which can cover up to 49% of construction costs under Peraturan Menteri Keuangan No. 68 Tahun 2024, or an availability payment — funds part of a project's cost directly, improving its financial viability upfront. A government guarantee (penjaminan pemerintah), issued by PT PII, is contingent: it only pays out if a specific government-side risk actually materializes. The two instruments come from different mechanisms and serve different purposes in the same project.

### What happens if the government contracting agency terminates the contract early?
If a PJPK terminates a KPBU contract early because of its own default, the contract's termination compensation formula determines what the project company is owed. Where that payment obligation falls within a guaranteed risk, PT PII can pay the business entity or its lenders if the PJPK does not, then exercises its regress right to recover the amount from the government party actually responsible. The guarantee bridges the payment gap; it does not erase the PJPK's underlying liability.

### Can a foreign company take part in an Indonesian PPP project?
Yes. Following Indonesia's investment-list reform under Peraturan Presiden No. 10 Tahun 2021, foreign investors can hold up to 100% ownership in most infrastructure sectors. In practice, foreign sponsor participation has grown slowly: the Asian Development Bank recorded 72 of 135 financially closed PPP projects with foreign sponsor participation as of 2019. Ownership rules are rarely the constraint — counterparty risk, IDR-denominated revenue, and uncertainty enforcing contracts in local courts weigh more heavily on foreign investment committees.

### What is a guarantee and regress agreement?
A guarantee and regress agreement is the contract PT PII signs for each project it guarantees. It sets out the guarantee itself and PT PII's regress right — the right to reclaim, from the government party actually responsible, any amount PT PII pays out on a claim. Regress means a guarantee payment is not a cost quietly absorbed by the state guarantor; it is recovered from whichever government entity caused the default, which is part of how the instrument protects fiscal discipline.

## Edge cases & nuance

- **Penjaminan ≠ subsidi.** A government guarantee does not fund a project directly and does not remove the business entity's commercial risk — it only pays if a defined government-side risk is triggered. Conflating it with Government Support (VGF/AP) is the single most common investor-side error the SERP gap analysis identified.
- **Force majeure is genuinely shared, not a clean row.** Most KPBU contracts treat natural force majeure as grounds for time relief (extension) for both parties rather than compensation; only certain politically-driven events (e.g., war, wide civil disturbance) tend to overlap with the government's assumed political risk. Present this as "partial," not as a binary yes/no — it is the one row in the matrix genuinely worth hedging in the prose, not just the table.
- **Regress means the state doesn't get a free pass.** A guarantee payout to a lender or project company does not close the book for government — PT PII can and does pursue the responsible government party for reimbursement. This is worth stating plainly for a lender audience assessing counterparty risk on the guarantee itself.
- **Two Kemenkeu-adjacent web properties currently disagree.** `jdih.kemenkeu.go.id` treats PMK 68/2024 as the current, consolidating regulation; `kpbu.kemenkeu.go.id`'s own guarantee-description page still lists the older PMK 260/2010 + PMK 8/2016 pairing without updating it. This is a real, observable inconsistency on the government's own web estate as at the access date — prefer the JDIH framing, and do not be surprised if a fact-checker finds the older citation still live elsewhere on a Kemenkeu-adjacent page.
- **Two funding-gap figures, two different stories.** Rp 1,905.3 trillion / Rp 753.11 trillion (RPJMN 2025–2029 base plan, mid-2025) and Rp 10,303 trillion (8%-growth-target scenario, a coordinating ministry estimate, December 2025) are not the same claim measured twice — they come from different planning exercises, different issuing bodies, and different dates. State scope and date every time either appears; never present one as an update of the other.
- **ADB's dataset is a decade-plus old at this point.** 2019 vintage figures (135 projects, USD 63,464 million, 72 foreign-sponsor projects) remain the standard citation used by many law firms and consultancies because ADB has not republished a newer count — but for an audience of credit analysts and DFI staff, dating it explicitly as "(2019 data)" is what separates a careful writer from a lazy one.
- **The guarantee cap/tenor/fee detail is market practice, not confirmed PT PII policy.** Every source for "capped, ~15-year tenor, upfront plus recurring six-monthly fees" traces back to law-firm advisory content, not a PT PII first-party publication independently verified this session — phrase it as how transaction advisers describe the instrument, not as PT PII's own stated terms.

## Definition snippet draft (55 words)

> Risk allocation in a public-private partnership is the contractual assignment of each project risk to the party best able to control and price it. In Indonesia's PPP scheme, known domestically as KPBU (Perpres 38/2015), government-side risks can be covered by a state guarantee; commercial risks, including demand and construction cost, stay with the project company.
