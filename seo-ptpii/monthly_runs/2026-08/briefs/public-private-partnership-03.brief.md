# Working Title (English)

**Public Private Partnership Guarantees: Indonesia's Cover vs MIGA and MDBs**

*(74 chars — H1 may run long; the constrained field is `meta_title`. Approved meta_title: "Public Private Partnership Guarantees Compared" (46 chars, keyword in first 26). Deliberately not "Everything You Need to Know About PPP Guarantees".)*

## Hook hypothesis

An international sponsor arriving in Indonesia already has a risk-mitigation vocabulary. They know what MIGA political risk insurance covers, they have priced a partial risk guarantee before, and their export credit agency has a view. What they do not know is where Indonesia's own government guarantee fits in that mental map — whether it substitutes for the instruments they already use, sits underneath them, or stacks alongside them. Get that wrong and you either double-pay for cover you already hold, or discover a gap at credit committee.

The SERP does not answer this. The World Bank and MIGA pages describe their own instruments; the Indonesian sources describe theirs; nobody puts them in one table. And there is a genuinely useful distinction buried in the World Bank's own material that almost never surfaces in Indonesian coverage: insurance-type political risk cover typically requires a waiting period and arbitration before it pays, while bank-type partial risk and credit guarantees from multilateral development banks do not. For a lender pricing liquidity risk on a debt service payment, that difference is the whole point.

PT PII can speak here without overreaching. It is the Ministry of Finance's Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur, it has worked alongside multilateral institutions to enlarge guarantee capacity for large projects, and it is externally rated — which makes it a comparable instrument rather than a black box.

## Article skeleton — H2s the writer should expand

Each H2 opens with a **direct 40–60-word answer** before elaboration (`seo_playbook.md` §2a).

1. **H2 (definitional)**: "What is a government guarantee in an Indonesian public private partnership?" — *covers*: cover issued over the risks that belong to the government contracting agency under the cooperation contract, raising the project's creditworthiness while ring-fencing the state's contingent liabilities; the scheme's Indonesian name, KPBU (*Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha*); and why it is a credit-enhancement instrument rather than insurance in the commercial sense.

2. **H2 (comparison / table — the centrepiece)**: "Indonesia's guarantee against the instruments you already use" — *covers*: a matrix with columns *Instrument · Who issues it · What it covers · Who is protected · How a claim is triggered · Typical use case*. Rows: (a) Indonesia's PPP government guarantee via the national guarantee provider; (b) MIGA political risk insurance; (c) multilateral development bank partial risk / credit guarantee; (d) export credit agency cover. Each cell must carry one claim only — this table is the article's citation asset.

3. **H2 (question-format)**: "Which instrument pays faster when something goes wrong?" — *covers*: the payment-mechanics distinction — a waiting period and arbitration are typical requirements of insurance-type cover, while bank-type partial risk and credit guarantees from MDBs carry no such waiting period. Explain why liquidity of payment, not just breadth of coverage, is what a lender is actually buying, and note honestly that the writer must present this as the general instrument-class distinction it is, not as a specific claim about any named product's terms.

4. **H2 (practical)**: "Can these instruments be combined on one project?" — *covers*: stacking and layering; the Indonesian co-guarantee arrangement where the government acts as co-guarantor for projects beyond the national provider's own capacity, with the joint guarantee administered by the provider; how a national guarantee covering contracting-agency obligations sits beneath political risk cover addressing sovereign-level events; and the practical warning about paying twice for overlapping cover.

5. **H2 (honest / counter-conventional — the trust-builder)**: "When you do not need Indonesia's government guarantee" — *covers*: where the payment obligor is not a government contracting agency; where the risk you are worried about is demand or construction cost, which no government guarantee reaches; where the concession is short enough that the guarantee's cap and tenor add little; and where a project's problem is a mis-specified risk allocation that no instrument will repair. Also note that guarantee capacity is finite and priced — it carries upfront and recurring fees and is capped per project.

6. **FAQ** — questions to answer (5–8):

   - Does Indonesia's PPP guarantee cover currency inconvertibility?
   - Is the Indonesian guarantee provider rated?
   - Can MIGA cover and an Indonesian government guarantee apply to the same project?
   - Is PPP the same as KPBU?
   - Who pays a claim under an Indonesian PPP guarantee, and can it be recovered?
   - What is a partial risk guarantee?

## Penjaminan infrastruktur / KPBU topic to teach

Teach vendor-neutrally: what a government guarantee is in the KPBU/PPP context and the four government-side risk families it reaches; what political risk insurance covers by contrast; what a partial risk guarantee is and how MDB-issued bank-type guarantees differ from insurance in claim mechanics; the co-guarantee structure; the regress mechanism, which means a paid claim is recoverable from the responsible party and the instrument is therefore not a transfer of cost to the state; and the ring-fencing purpose — protecting the state budget from sudden claim shocks.
