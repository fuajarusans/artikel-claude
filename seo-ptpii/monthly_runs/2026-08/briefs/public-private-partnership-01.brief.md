**Risk Allocation in Indonesia's Public Private Partnership: Who Carries What**

## Hook hypothesis

A foreign sponsor evaluating an Indonesian toll road or water project can read the whole regulatory stack and still not answer the only question their investment committee asks: **if this goes wrong, who pays?** The top-ranking results are either a USD 43 ADB monitor, a World Bank gap analysis, or a law-firm capability page. None of them lay out the risk matrix side by side with what a government guarantee actually covers and — more importantly — what it deliberately does not. The client's own hub page names "risk mitigation for investors" but never draws the line between government-side obligations and the sponsor's own commercial exposure. That gap is the article.

PT PII can speak to this credibly because it is the only Indonesian state-owned enterprise mandated as Badan Usaha Penjaminan Infrastruktur, appraising and structuring exactly this allocation before it will issue a guarantee. Its appraisal role is the first-party experience signal: it is the institution that says no when a risk has been mis-assigned.

## Article skeleton — H2s the writer should expand

Each H2 opens with a **direct 40–60-word answer** before elaboration (GEO, `seo_playbook.md` §2a).

1. **H2 (definitional)**: "What is risk allocation in an Indonesian public private partnership?" — *covers*: the principle that each risk sits with the party best able to control and price it; PPP as the international term and KPBU (*Kerja Sama Pemerintah dengan Badan Usaha*) as its Indonesian name; the two contracting parties — the PJPK (*Penanggung Jawab Proyek Kerja Sama*, the government contracting agency) and the Badan Usaha Pelaksana (the project company). Anchor entity: Perpres 38/2015, only if verified in force.

2. **H2 (practical / table — the centrepiece)**: "Who carries which risk across the project life" — *covers*: a table with columns *Risk category · Typically borne by · Reachable by a government guarantee?* Rows to include at minimum: land acquisition and site availability; permits and approvals; discriminatory, project-specific change of law; tariff adjustment not delivered as contracted; construction cost overrun and delay; demand / traffic / volume; operating cost; foreign exchange and IDR revenue denomination; early termination by government default; force majeure. The writer must mark each cell honestly, including the "no" rows.

3. **H2 (question-format)**: "What does Indonesia's government guarantee actually cover?" — *covers*: the four government-side risk families in `company_profile.md` — discriminatory project-specific change of law, delay or failure to obtain key government approvals, early termination on PJPK default, and other acts or omissions of government as contract counterparty. Add the practical shape from the SERP research (subject to verification): coverage typically capped, tenor of roughly up to 15 years, upfront and recurring fees. Distinguish clearly, in one sentence each, between **Government Support** (Viability Gap Fund, availability payment, land funding) and **Government Guarantee** — these are different instruments from different parts of the state.

4. **H2 (honest / counter-conventional — the trust-builder)**: "What the guarantee will not do for you" — *covers*: it does not cover the business entity's commercial risk; it is not a subsidy and not a revenue floor; it does not price demand risk; it will not rescue a project whose allocation was wrong at signing; and it carries **regress** — the guarantee provider has the right to recover paid claims from the responsible party, so the government contracting agency remains economically liable. Also state the honest market fact that the Indonesian PPP market remains dominated by local investors and financiers, with foreign participation growing slowly.

5. **H2 (decision / practical)**: "How to test your own risk matrix before you bid" — *covers*: a short numbered checklist an adviser can run — is each risk assigned to the party that controls it; is every government obligation expressed as a payable amount with a trigger; is the termination compensation formula bankable; is the guarantee's cap and tenor matched to the debt tenor; does anything in the matrix depend on a regulation younger than the concession.

## Penjaminan infrastruktur / KPBU topic to teach

Teach vendor-neutrally: the risk-allocation principle; the PJPK–Badan Usaha contractual relationship; the taxonomy of infrastructure risk across pre-construction, construction and operation; the boundary between government-side risk and commercial risk; the regress mechanism; and the fact that a guarantee is a **credit-enhancement and contingent-liability management tool**, not a transfer of business risk. Explain KPBU as Indonesia's term for PPP on first mention and then use both naturally.
