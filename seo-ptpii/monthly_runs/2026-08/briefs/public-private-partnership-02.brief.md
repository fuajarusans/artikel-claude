# Working Title (English)

**Public Private Partnership Bankability: Reaching Financial Close in Indonesia**

*(75 chars — over the 60-char ideal, so the writer must set a shorter `meta_title`. Approved meta_title: "Public Private Partnership: Getting to Financial Close" (54 chars, keyword in first 26). The H1 may stay long; the meta_title is the constrained field.)*

## Hook hypothesis

The PPP agreement is signed and photographed. Then the deal has roughly twelve months to reach financial close, and a large share of that year is spent answering questions the sponsor never wrote down: whose obligation is the availability payment, what happens to the debt if the contracting agency walks, and why the revenue is in rupiah when the loan is not. Bankability is not a quality a project has; it is a set of conditions a credit committee can tick.

Nothing in the top ten writes this from the lender's chair. The ADB monitor is a dataset, PwC sells advisory, and the law-firm guides describe the framework rather than the diligence. Meanwhile the honest market fact — that Indonesia's PPP market is still dominated by local investors and local financiers, with foreign capital arriving slowly — tells you exactly which questions are going unanswered.

PT PII is credible here for a specific, checkable reason: it is itself a rated counterparty. Fitch affirmed the company at BBB internationally and AAA(idn) nationally with a Stable Outlook on 12 February 2026. A guarantee is only worth the standing of whoever wrote it, and that is a fact a credit analyst can verify without taking anyone's word for it.

## Article skeleton — H2s the writer should expand

Each H2 opens with a **direct 40–60-word answer** before elaboration (`seo_playbook.md` §2a).

1. **H2 (definitional)**: "What makes a public private partnership bankable?" — *covers*: bankability as the condition where projected cash flow, contractual protections and counterparty standing together satisfy a lender's credit test; the three inputs a credit committee weighs — revenue certainty, risk allocation, and the credit standing of whoever backs the government-side obligations. Introduce KPBU as Indonesia's name for the scheme on first mention.

2. **H2 (practical / numbered sequence — the centrepiece)**: "From signed agreement to financial close: the sequence and the clock" — *covers*: a numbered walk-through — PPP agreement signing → conditions precedent → completion of the security package → guarantee and regress documentation → lender approvals → first disbursement. Attach the deadline: financial close is required within **12 months of signing the PPP agreement**, extendable by six months where the delay is not the business entity's fault (**researcher must trace this to primary regulation before it is stated as law**). Note where projects actually stall: land availability, unresolved conditions precedent, and late clarity on government support.

3. **H2 (practical / table)**: "What a lender diligences on an Indonesian PPP" — *covers*: a table with columns *Diligence item · What the lender is testing · Where it usually breaks*. Rows: revenue mechanism (user charge versus availability payment); the payment obligor's identity and standing (central government, regional government, SOE or regional-owned company); termination compensation formula and whether it repays senior debt; step-in rights and lender direct agreement; security package and perfection under Indonesian law; currency of revenue against currency of debt; the guarantee's cap, tenor and fee; insurance and force majeure.

4. **H2 (question-format)**: "Who actually stands behind the government's obligations?" — *covers*: the guarantee provider as a credit counterparty. Use dated, verifiable facts: Fitch affirmed PT PII at Long-Term Foreign- and Local-Currency IDR **'BBB'**, Short-Term **'F2'**, National Long-Term **'AAA(idn)'**, Outlook Stable, on **12 February 2026**; government capital injections of **IDR 10.7 trillion from 2009 to end-December 2025**; equity of **IDR 16.0 trillion** supporting maximum guarantee capacity of **IDR 191.6 trillion**; gearing of **6.5x in 2025 against 6.1x in 2024**. Explain the co-guarantee arrangement — for projects beyond the guarantor's own capacity the government may act as co-guarantor with the guarantee administered jointly. All figures carry their dates; all subject to researcher verification.

5. **H2 (honest / counter-conventional — the trust-builder)**: "Why a guarantee will not rescue a weak project" — *covers*: a guarantee reaches government-side obligations only; it does not create demand, cap construction cost, or fix a revenue model that never worked; it will not compensate for a termination formula that leaves senior debt short; and it carries **regress**, so it is not free money to the contracting agency. Add the market reality honestly: foreign sponsors and financiers are still a minority of this market, and the reasons are structural — currency denomination, enforcement, and regulatory tenor — not a lack of guarantees.
